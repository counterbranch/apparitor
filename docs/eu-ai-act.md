# EU AI Act: authorization evidence reference

Current to **9 October 2026**. This is a technical mapping, not a legal conclusion.
Classification depends on the complete AI system and its intended use; an authorization
component is not automatically a high-risk AI system.

## Current law and dates

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 entered into force on 1 August 2024. Prohibitions and the
original AI-literacy provision applied from 2 February 2025; governance and general-purpose
AI provisions applied from 2 August 2025. The AI Omnibus entered into force on 27 July
2026 and moved the principal high-risk dates: Annex III systems apply from **2 December
2027**, and high-risk systems embedded in Annex I products apply from **2 August 2028**.
Other provisions can already apply. Check the current consolidated text and transition
rules rather than relying on the Act as originally published.

Official sources:

## Role and obligation boundaries

Article 12 requires a provider to design a high-risk system with logging capabilities that
enable event recording over the system's lifetime. The events must support traceability
appropriate to the system's intended purpose, including identifying risk situations and
supporting post-market monitoring. Article 19 requires providers to keep automatically
generated logs under their control for an appropriate period, normally at least six months.
Article 26(6) separately requires deployers to keep logs automatically generated by the
high-risk system under their control for an appropriate period, normally at least six
months. Article 14 governs provider-designed human-oversight measures; Article 26 assigns
deployers operational duties for human oversight.

These are role-specific duties. Article 12 does not itself require every event to contain
a user identity, policy version, latency, raw input, protected attribute, or execution
result. It also does not state a general tamper-evidence requirement. Integrity controls,
access restrictions, retention and deletion belong to the deployment's evidence system
and may follow from other duties or laws.

## Privacy and evidence design

The stable C1/C2/C3 grammar is documented in [audit-log.md](audit-log.md). C1 and C2 can
contain direct subject identifiers and must be treated as sensitive. The optional evidence
records in [compliance-evidence.md](compliance-evidence.md) instead require host-created,
tenant-scoped opaque references and explicitly report categories not collected.

An authorization `allow` is not proof that a tool executed or succeeded. apparitor emits
`execution_status=not_observed` for decision evidence. A host may later attach a separately
observed execution outcome. `human_review` proves only that authorization requested
oversight; it does not prove who reviewed the action or the result.

Prefer keyed, tenant-specific pseudonyms with managed key rotation. Ordinary hashes of
emails or account IDs are vulnerable to guessing. Keep the re-identification mapping out
of the evidence stream, apply role-based access, define retention and deletion rules, and
avoid collecting protected attributes merely to populate a compliance record.
